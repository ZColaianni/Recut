How Recut works

This section gets updated as the app changes, so it always matches what's actually built.

Dashboard

Your at-a-glance summary: today's calorie target, calories left, your current phase and how many days you've been in it, and your latest weight with its 7-day trend. Nothing is entered here; it just reflects what's set up everywhere else.

Profile & macros

Enter your sex, age, weight, height, and activity level, then pick a phase: Cut (fat loss, calorie deficit), Maintain (hold steady), Refeed (short surplus to reset), or Lean bulk (slow muscle gain). This calculates your daily calorie and macro targets using the Mifflin-St Jeor formula, and those targets drive everything else in the app. Log your weight here too; it feeds your dashboard trend and plateau detection.

Food log

Search a food (pulls real macros from a public food database), scan a barcode, or type numbers in by hand. Add several items to build a meal, then hit "Save meal to log" to commit them all at once, grouped by Breakfast/Lunch/Dinner/Pre-workout/Snack. This is the flexible option; use it for anything outside your usual plan.

Meal plan

For foods you already eat consistently (say, from a coach). Enter each food once, tagged as a protein, carb, or fat source, or "fixed" if it never changes, and Recut resizes the portions to hit your current phase's targets automatically. Import a CSV, Excel, or PDF plan instead of typing it in, if you have one. One click adds the whole scaled day to your food log. The Grocery list below it is a plain checklist of everything on your plan, grouped by meal. Quantities aren't shown since plan foods are entered by weight but bought in packages, and one doesn't reliably convert to the other.

Meal ideas

For when you don't have a fixed plan and don't know what to eat. Add foods you like per meal, and "Suggest something" finds whichever one is closest to your remaining calories for the day. Good for someone who wants flexibility without decision fatigue.

Check-ins

Weekly log of a date, weight, optional note, and a progress photo. Kept separate from daily logging: it's meant to be a slower, occasional record rather than something you touch every day. Once you've got two or more photos saved, Compare progress below it lets you pick any two dates and view them side by side, with the weight change and days between them.

Plateau detection

If you're cutting or bulking and your weight hasn't moved much over 14+ days, a banner appears on the dashboard suggesting a small calorie adjustment. It never changes anything on its own; you choose to apply it or dismiss it.

How it's stored

Everything lives in this browser only: no account, no server, no syncing across devices yet. Clearing your browser data will erase it. Photos are stored separately from the rest of your data since they're larger. The app also ships with the groundwork to install like a real app and work offline once it's hosted somewhere; none of that does anything while you're just opening the file directly.
